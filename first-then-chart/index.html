First-Then Chart, Schedule or Board: Examples

Last updated: November 26, 2026

A first-then chart, a first/then schedule and a First-Then board are three names for the same tool: two pictures in a row, first a task, then something the child wants. This page shows what it looks like, gives 18 examples, and explains how to make one.

Is a first-then chart the same as a First-Then board?

Yes. The tool is the same, and only the name changes. You may see it called a first-then chart, a “first and then” board or a first/then schedule.

A training guide from the University of North Carolina uses the name first/then schedule and describes it as two events in a row that help a student understand what comes next (see Sources). On this site we say First-Then board.

What does a first-then chart look like?

It has two boxes side by side with an arrow between them. The left box says First and shows the task. The right box says Then and shows the thing the child wants.

  • Each box holds one picture and, if you like, one or two words.
  • The picture can be a photo, a drawing or an icon. For a child who reads, it can be a word only.
  • When the task is done, the First box gets a check mark, and the child gets what is in the Then box.

A chart with three boxes is called First-Then-Next. Use it later, when two boxes work well.

What can I put on a first-then chart?

Put one small task in the First box and one thing your child really wants in the Then box. Here are 18 examples for different parts of the day.

  • MorningFirst get dressed, then breakfast · First brush teeth, then play · First shoes on, then car
  • MealsFirst wash hands, then snack · First sit down, then juice · First set the table, then dessert
  • School and homeworkFirst write your name, then computer · First homework, then tablet · First clean up, then stickers
  • Going outFirst coat on, then park · First hold hands, then playground · First shopping, then bubbles
  • Therapy and learningFirst say five words, then puzzle · First table work, then swing · First listen, then music
  • EveningFirst bath, then story · First pajamas, then teddy bear · First toys in the box, then TV

Change the words so that they fit your child. A good task is short and clear. A good reward is something your child wants today, not something you think they should want.

Should the chart be on paper or on a screen?

Use the one your child looks at. You can also use both.

  • Paper works where a screen is a problem: in the bathroom, in the car, on a school desk. You can download a blank first-then chart as a PDF and add your own pictures.
  • A screen is faster to change. On the board on this site you change a card in a few seconds, and the board shows a check mark when the task is done.

Is a first/then schedule a visual schedule?

It is the shortest kind of visual schedule. A first/then schedule shows two steps. A full visual schedule shows many steps, for example the whole morning or the whole school day.

Start with two steps: two pictures are easy to understand. When that goes well, you can add a third card, and later a longer schedule. The guide First-Then board vs visual schedule vs token board explains when to use which.

Why can it help?

A picture stays in place after the words have been said. The child can look at the chart again and see what is asked now and what comes after.

The order of the two boxes follows the Premack principle: something the child enjoys comes right after something they enjoy less, and that makes the first task easier to start. Visual supports like this one are also among the 28 evidence-based practices for autistic children and young people that the National Clearinghouse on Autism Evidence and Practice named in its 2020 review (see Sources).

How do I make a first-then chart?

You can make one on this site in about two minutes. It is free, and you need no account.

  1. Open the board and tap the pencil on the First card.
  2. Pick an icon or type a word for one small task.
  3. Tap the pencil on the Then card and pick the reward.
  4. Show the chart to your child and say it in a few words: “First shoes on, then park.”
  5. When the task is done, tap the First card and give the reward right away.

First-Then Board for Speech Therapy and ABA

Last updated: November 26, 2026

In a therapy session, a First-Then board shows the child the plan in two pictures: first the work, then the fun thing. It can make the session easier to follow, it gives you a natural reason to practice words, and the family can use the same board at home.

Why do therapists use a First-Then board?

It can make the session easier to understand. The child sees that the work has an end and that something good comes after it.

A First-Then board is a visual support, and the reward on the Then card is a form of reinforcement. Visual supports and reinforcement are both on the list of 28 evidence-based practices for autistic children and young people in the 2020 review by the National Clearinghouse on Autism Evidence and Practice. In a University of North Carolina training guide the tool is called a first/then schedule, and it is described as two events in a row that help the student understand what comes next (see Sources).

The order of the cards follows the Premack principle: something the child enjoys comes right after the work, and that can make the work easier to start.

How do I use it in a session?

Set up the board before the child sits down, and keep it in view during the whole activity. The steps are the same in speech therapy, ABA, occupational therapy or tutoring.

  1. Let the child choose the Then. Offer two or three things, so the Then is something the child really wants today.
  2. Put one short task on First. For example five words, one page or one turn in a game.
  3. Show and say. Point to each card: “First say five words, then bubbles.”
  4. Mark the end. When the task is done, tap the First card so the check mark shows. Say “Finished.”
  5. Give the Then right away. Keep it short, one or two minutes, and then set up the next pair.

With the plus button under the cards you can prepare several pairs before the session and move through them one by one.

What can I put on the cards?

Put a small, countable piece of work on First and a short, real reward on Then.

  • Speech and languageFirst say five words, then bubbles · First name the pictures, then puzzle piece · First say the sentence, then ball
  • Table workFirst three cards, then swing · First match the colors, then music · First sit down, then tablet
  • Play and turn-takingFirst my turn, then your turn · First wait, then go · First clean up, then new game

Which words can I practice with the board?

The board itself gives you words to model in every session. Say them the same way each time.

  • “First” and “then”: the order of two things.
  • “Finished” or “all done”, when you tap the First card.
  • The name of the reward: the child can ask for it by word, sign or picture before they get it.
  • “My turn” and “your turn”, when the Then is a game for two.

You decide what fits the child’s goals. These are common ideas, not a program.

Photos, icons or words?

Choose the kind of picture this child understands best. The training guide lists these forms a visual support can take: real objects, photographs, drawings or picture symbols, and written words.

For a child who is new to pictures, a photo of the real toy or the real room is often easier than an icon. If the child reads, you can use words only.

Does it work in online sessions?

Yes. Open the board in your browser and share that window. The child and the parent see the same two cards, and you tap the First card when the task is done.

On a tablet in the room, use full screen. The child then sees only the board and cannot change the cards.

What should I send home?

Send the same board, with the same pictures and the same words. The board is easier to follow when it looks the same in the session and at home.

  • Show the parent how you say it: one short sentence, and the reward right away.
  • Give them a printed blank board with the same pictures drawn or glued on, or the link to this site.
  • Ask them to start with easy tasks at home, not with the hardest moment of the day.

Can I use it together with a token board?

Yes. You can put the token board on the First card: “First five tokens, then tablet.” The child earns the tokens during the work and gets the Then when the board is full.

With tokens the reward comes later, so start with a plain First-Then board and add tokens when it works well. The guide First-Then board vs visual schedule vs token board explains the difference. Our app for token boards is Token Board.
